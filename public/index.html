Meridian

A society of celestial cartography and instrument-making keeps its autumn exhibition open after dark, with engraved atlases, working astrolabes, and the sky itself turning on the wall.

The twenty named stars are plotted from catalogue positions (right ascension and declination, J2000); the constellation figures between them are drawn as line sets, not as the modern boundaries, and the faint field stars are generated for texture.

Text description of the planisphere

The chart rotates about the north celestial pole. Principal stars are plotted at their catalogued positions and joined into six line-drawn constellations: Ursa Major, Orion, Cassiopeia, Cygnus, Lyra and Scorpius. The position of the sun is marked for the selected month.

  • Ursa Major, seven principal stars; Dubhe and Merak point to the pole.
  • Orion, eight principal stars with the Belt across the middle.
  • Cassiopeia, five principal stars forming the chair.
  • Cygnus, five principal stars forming the swan, Deneb at the tail.
  • Lyra, five principal stars; Vega is the brightest in the northern summer sky.
  • Scorpius, seven principal stars; Antares is the heart of the scorpion.

The society

Meridian has kept its rooms at Observatory Passage since 1776, when a group of engravers took over a workshop that had been cutting instruments for the Admiralty and began printing the charts they had been measuring against.

The two trades have stayed in the same building ever since. Plates are drawn by hand from a sight taken on the roof, and every instrument in the collection was built to answer a question that a plate had already asked in ink. The autumn exhibition puts both halves of that argument in the same room.

The rooms are open to the public from late afternoon until the last session ends, and a visit is led by an attendant rather than left to a leaflet. Nothing on the wall is behind glass, and the instruments come out of their cases on the bench.

What is on show

Four rooms make up the exhibition, in the order a visitor walks them.

01 Plates Six engraved charts on the wall, and the reference stars they were drawn from. 02 Instruments The engraver's bench, the astrolabe and the error notebooks that corrected them. 03 Programme Opening nights, bench demonstrations, moon studies and the closing late views. 04 Visit Opening hours, access, group visits and the reservation desk.

An evening in the Long Room

Doors open fifteen minutes before the session. The attendant takes the bench first and the instruments are passed round while the light is still bright enough to read a graduation by. The plates come next, lit from the side so the burin cuts cast their own shadows.

The evening finishes at the north wall, where the planisphere turns through a year in the time it takes to ask a question about it.
